Does spreading content wear it out?

Brands push the same post to one retailer, sometimes to hundreds. We pulled a year of Facebook and Instagram shares to find out what actually happens as it spreads.

01 — THE OBVIOUS READ

The more retailers post it, the worse it does.

Rank every retailer who shares the same piece of content by when they posted it, then average engagement at each rank. The line drops fast — by the 21st+ retailer, engagement is down 45–65% from the first post. Instagram falls even harder than Facebook.

02 — BUT CHECK ONE PIECE OF CONTENT AT A TIME

Run the same test per piece of content. The line disappears.

Instead of pooling everything, take one specific share setting and check whether its own engagement trends down as more retailers post it. Do that for every piece of content with 5+ posts, and the result is close to a coin flip.

03 — WHY THE POOLED LINE STILL DROPS

It's not decay. It's a change in who's left in the room.

Every piece of content has a "1st retailer," so that bucket includes everything — including high-performing one-off campaigns. Only content popular enough to reach 21+ retailers shows up that far out, and that's disproportionately generic, always-on auto-share material that was lower-engagement from day one.

04 — MANUAL VS. AUTO-SHARE

Manual posting holds up much better.

Split by share mode and the gap is stark: manual posts keep meaningfully higher engagement across every retailer-order bucket than auto-share — evidence the composition effect, not fatigue, is doing the work.

05 — WHAT ABOUT CALENDAR TIME?

Flat for a month, then it ticks up.

Bucket by days since a piece of content's first-ever post instead of retailer count. Engagement stays roughly flat through day 30 on both modes, then rises in the 31+ day bucket — the opposite of decay. Content still running a month later is the content that was working.

06 — THE ONE REAL FATIGUE SIGNAL

A retailer's own 4th repost is where it actually breaks.

A retailer's 2nd post of identical content performs about the same as their 1st — on Instagram, even better. The real drop is at the 4th+ post of the same content: 30–65% below their first try, on both channels.

4th+ own repost = the actual fatigue point
07 — FORMAT, INDUSTRY, REACH

The channels want opposite things.

Facebook does slightly better with photo. Instagram does much better with video — over 2x the impressions. Automotive content reaches the widest network (107 retailers/setting on average); Luxury Jewelry, Watches and Tactical brands post the highest engagement, led by one standout: Smith & Wesson, at 9.7 avg. engagement per setting.

The takeaway

Spreading content doesn't wear it out. Reposting the same thing does.

Read the pooled decline and the per-content test together — one tells you what's in the room, the other tells you whether any single piece of content is actually losing steam. It isn't. The real signal worth acting on is much narrower: a retailer's 4th+ repost of identical content.

Don't

Read the retailer-order decline chart alone and conclude wider distribution degrades content.

Do

Flag 4th+ identical reposts by the same retailer as the one real fatigue signal worth surfacing.

Match format to channel

Video on Instagram, photo on Facebook — the two channels reward opposite formats.

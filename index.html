Does spreading content wear it out?

Short answer: not really — but something else does. We pulled a year of Facebook and Instagram shares to find out what actually happens as a post spreads.

AT FIRST GLANCE

The more retailers who share something, the worse it seems to perform.

Every time a retailer posts a piece of content, we can count how many other retailers already posted that same thing. Line up every post by that count and average how well each group does — the line drops fast. By the time a post has been shared by its 21st+ retailer, it's down 45–65% from the very first share.

BUT HERE'S THE CATCH

Follow one piece of content at a time, and the decline disappears.

That first chart mixes thousands of different posts together. Some are one-off campaigns that only ever reach a couple of retailers — often high performers. Others are old, always-available content that gets reused by hundreds of retailers over months — and it's popular precisely because it's safe and generic, not because it was ever a top performer. When you instead follow one single piece of content and ask "does its own engagement drop as more retailers pick it up," the answer is close to a coin flip.

SO WHAT ACTUALLY CAUSES FATIGUE?

The same retailer posting the exact same thing, over and over.

Track one retailer re-sharing identical content. Their 2nd time posting it does just as well as their 1st — on Instagram, even a little better. It's not until the 4th time posting the same content that performance really drops: 30–65% below their first try, on both channels. That's the one place fatigue is real.

A FEW MORE PATTERNS BONUS

Three smaller things worth knowing.

  • ＋Manual beats auto-share. Posts shared by hand hold up noticeably better than auto-shared ones as they spread — the same story as above: it's what's being posted, not the act of sharing it, that matters.
  • ＋Time doesn't wear it out either. Performance stays flat for about a month, then ticks back up. Content still being shared a month later is the content that was working.
  • ＋The channels want different things. Facebook does slightly better with photos; Instagram does much better with video — more than double the reach.
The takeaway

Spreading content doesn't wear it out. Reposting the exact same thing does.

The decline in that first chart isn't fatigue — it's an illusion caused by averaging very different kinds of content together. No individual post actually wears out as more retailers share it. The one pattern worth acting on is much narrower: a retailer posting identical content a 4th time or more.

Don't

Look at a chart like the first one and conclude that spreading content to more retailers makes it perform worse.

Do

Watch for a retailer reposting identical content a 4th+ time — that's the one real fatigue signal worth flagging.
